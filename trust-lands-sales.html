Trust Lands Sales

Utah's Trust Lands Administration sells and leases state trust land to earn money for public schools. This map shows parcels sold at auction, parcels listed for the next auction, and land optioned for development, with the price and acreage from the public record. It starts with the parcels the Hive has documented and grows as more are added.

Words on this map

Tap a card to flip it.

Trust landsTap to flip ↻
Land the federal government gave Utah at statehood to earn money for public schools and other named beneficiaries. The Trust Lands Administration (once called SITLA) sells, leases and develops it, and the money goes into permanent funds.
Land sales auctionTap to flip ↻
How the Trust Lands Administration sells parcels to the public. It holds regular auctions and must seek full value for the schools and institutions it serves.
Minimum bidTap to flip ↻
The lowest price the trust will accept for a parcel at auction. "TBD" means it hadn't been set yet.
Option agreementTap to flip ↻
A promise to make a deal later if conditions are met by a deadline. Nothing is leased or sold until the conditions are met.
Development leaseTap to flip ↻
A lease, not a sale. The developer builds on trust land for a set term, the trust keeps the land, and the trust is paid under the lease terms.
Parcel numberTap to flip ↻
The trust's own ID for a parcel, such as C-26957. It is how a parcel is listed in auction results.
Zoning exemptionTap to flip ↻
Utah law lets trust land sit outside county zoning while the state owns it. Some counties, like Garfield, zone it only after it's sold.
Permanent school fundTap to flip ↻
The fund trust land money flows into. It is invested, and the earnings go to public schools each year.

What this map shows so far

This first version shows the trust land deals already documented in Hive case files, plus sale rows read from the Trust Lands auction page. The full auction history lists many more parcels; they'll be added as the list is reviewed.

The auction page lists the parcel, county, acreage and price, but not the buyer.

Pin locations

Most auction rows give a county, not a location. Those pins sit in the county or nearest town, and each one says so. They don't mark parcel boundaries.
